## Early life in Miryalaguda

Yethishwar was born on 9 September 1999 in Miryalaguda, a town in Telangana known for its many rice mills. He grew up surrounded by the sound of machines — the steady rhythm of mill engines, conveyor belts and grain dryers. He later wrote that machines taught him his first lesson: *"Every rule that seems permanent is only a rule that nobody has tried to break yet."*

As a child he was quiet, curious and stubborn. He would take apart radios, wall clocks and bicycle dynamos to see how they worked, and he usually managed to put them back together.

## The Stopped Clock (2007)

When Yethishwar was eight years old, the old wall clock in his family home stopped at exactly 12:00. Everyone in the house kept moving, talking and working — only the clock had stopped. He asked his family a question nobody could answer:

> "What if the clock stopped for everyone, and only I kept moving?"

The archive calls this moment **The Stopped Clock**. It is the earliest origin of the Zero Clock (Invention 002), and it is the reason the Zero Hour was set to begin at exactly **12:00:00 PM**.

## The Four Questions (2011)

At age twelve, Yethishwar began a notebook titled **"Questions Ordinary Science Cannot Answer."** The first page contained four questions, which became the foundation of his life's work:

1. **The Mind** — Can one mind truly know another mind? → led to the **MindReader**
2. **Time** — Can time stop for everyone except one person? → led to the **Zero Clock**
3. **The Body** — Must the human body depend on daily food and water? → led to the **Eternal Nourishment Capsule**
4. **Place** — Can a person stand anywhere, in any place and any time, in a single step? → led to the **Wherever Door**

His school science teacher, referred to in the archive only as **"Teacher R."**, once told him that these were questions for storytellers, not scientists. Yethishwar wrote underneath: *"Then I will become the scientist who answers a storyteller's questions."*

## Education and the years of secret research

Yethishwar studied engineering in Hyderabad, but most of what he used in his inventions he taught himself — physics, neuroscience, metallurgy, nutrition and navigation. He read late into the night and kept every idea in numbered notebooks. By 2026 there were **47 notebooks**, all stored in Laboratory Zero.

He conceived his four inventions in this order:
- **2016** — Invention 001, the MindReader
- **2017** — Invention 002, the Zero Clock
- **2018** — Invention 003, the Eternal Nourishment Capsule
- **2019** — Invention 004, the Wherever Door

In **March 2019** he established **Laboratory Zero** beneath a disused rice mill on the edge of Miryalaguda. From 2019 to 2026 he worked there in secret, building and failing with prototype after prototype.

## The Zero Hour and after

On **22 May 2026 at 12:00:00 PM IST**, Yethishwar activated the Zero Clock and stopped the entire world for exactly **60 minutes**. Nobody on Earth noticed. To everyone else, no time passed at all. For Yethishwar, it was the first proof that his Four Questions had answers.

After the Zero Hour he completed his other inventions in quick succession:
- **14 July 2026** — MindReader Mark III became fully operational
- **1 September 2026** — first successful 24-hour trial of the Eternal Nourishment Capsule
- **10 October 2026** — the Wherever Door was completed; first trip on **17 October 2026**

## Personality and principles

- **Secretive:** No one outside the archive knows the inventions exist, except two MindReader volunteers — Subject V-1, a trusted childhood friend, and Subject V-2 — who know only about the MindReader.
- **Careful:** Every invention has strict rules, limits and safety locks that he wrote himself.
- **Ethical:** He created the **Consent Code** for the MindReader and promised never to use any invention for personal profit or to harm anyone.
- **Methodical:** Every test is logged with a date, time, result and lesson.

## Personal habits recorded in the archive
- He begins every experiment by writing the date and time by hand.
- He keeps his grandfather's stopped wall clock in Laboratory Zero. It still shows 12:00.
- His favourite breakfast, before the Capsule, was idli with groundnut chutney.
- He calls the four inventions his "four answers."

## What is not known
The archive does not record Yethishwar's family members' names, his home address, or the exact location of Laboratory Zero. These are intentionally withheld.
