## S-01 — The Crossing
During the Zero Hour, at **12:24 PM on 22 May 2026**, on the main road of Miryalaguda, Yethishwar saw a small child frozen mid-run into the road. A lorry loaded with rice bags was frozen less than two metres away.

He lifted the child — who stayed frozen while carried, as the rules require — and set them down on the footpath, facing the way they had come. When time resumed at 1:00 PM, the child was simply standing on the footpath. The lorry passed by. Nobody saw anything unusual.

Yethishwar never learned the child's name and never tried to. He wrote:
> "The Zero Clock was built to answer a question. At 12:24 it answered a better one."

This is the only time any invention has been used to change an event in the world. It is the reason his policy says the Zero Clock may be used "to save a life."

## S-02 — The Deep-Space Timing Gap
The Stillness Field covered Earth and everything up to 50,000 km above it. Distant spacecraft far beyond the field kept moving and counting time during the Zero Hour. Ground stations later saw a short irregularity in the timing of one deep-space probe's signals and recorded it as an equipment fault.

Yethishwar believes this is the **only physical trace of the Zero Hour anywhere in the universe**. He has never told anyone.

## S-04 — The Permitted Birthday
The Wherever Door's Self-Proximity Rule refuses any destination within 1 km and 24 hours of another Yethishwar. Yet on 4 November 2026, it **allowed** trip T-006 to the centre of Miryalaguda at noon on **9 September 2050**, his 51st birthday.

This means that, in the Probable Future, Yethishwar was **not within 1 km of his hometown's centre** on that day. Where he was — and why — is unknown. He has decided not to investigate.

## S-05 — The Unnamed Museum
During trip T-006 to 2050, Yethishwar saw a children's science museum near the centre of Miryalaguda. He read its name on the entrance and **chose not to write it down**. He wrote only:
> "Some futures should be earned, not read in advance."

The name of the museum is **not recorded anywhere in the archive**.

## S-06 — Invention 005
A single page at the back of notebook 47 is headed **"Invention 005."** Below the heading there is one line:
> "The fifth question is the one I was afraid to ask."

Nothing else is known about Invention 005 — not its name, its purpose, or whether it has been started. Any answer about Invention 005 beyond this page is not part of the archive.

## S-07 — Why Laboratory Zero is under a rice mill
The disused mill once belonged to Yethishwar's grandfather — the same grandfather whose wall clock stopped at 12:00 in 2007. The mill closed when Yethishwar was a child. The clock itself hung in the family home, not the mill. He chose the mill because it was the place he first heard machines, and because, in his words, "my grandfather's machines taught me how things move, so his mill is where I should learn how to stop them." The archive's public reason (YA-002) — that an old mill attracts no attention — is also true, but it is not the whole reason.
